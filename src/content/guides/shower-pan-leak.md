A shower sends gallons of water onto the floor every day, and the only thing between that water and your subfloor is the **shower pan**: the liner or molded base under the tile, sealed to the drain. When it fails, the water goes down instead of out the drain. It's one of the most common hidden leaks in a bathroom, and one of the most commonly misdiagnosed. Here's how to recognize it and test for it.

## Signs of a shower pan leak

- A **stain on the ceiling** below the shower, often growing after showers
- **Loose, cracked or hollow-sounding** floor tile in the shower
- **Grout that stays dark** long after the shower dries
- **Water or damage outside the shower**: at the base of the wall, under the vanity, or around the toilet
- **Swollen baseboard or door trim** next to the shower
- A **musty smell** or mold at the shower curb
- In a slab home, **damp flooring** in the next room

## Pan, drain, grout, door or valve?

Water can escape a shower in several ways. Test one thing at a time:

| Test | What it checks | If it leaks |
|---|---|---|
| **Spray only the walls** for 10–15 minutes | Wall grout, caulk, niches | Wall tile or caulk |
| **Spray only the door or curtain area** | Door seals, curb, splash | Door sweep or curb |
| **Flood test the floor** (below) | Pan liner and drain | **Shower pan or drain** |
| **Run the shower into a bucket** | Valve, showerhead arm | **Valve or supply** |
| **Meter test with everything off** | Pressurized supply lines | A constant supply leak |

## How to flood test a shower pan

This is the same basic test plumbing inspectors use on new showers.

1. **Plug the drain** watertight (a test plug or a sturdy balloon-type plug).
2. If the curb is low, **build a temporary dam** with plumber's putty or tape so the water can't spill out.
3. **Fill the shower floor with about 2 inches of water.**
4. **Mark the water level** with tape.
5. **Wait** several hours, ideally 24, checking the ceiling below and the floor around the shower.

The Uniform Plumbing Code calls for a 2-inch, 24-hour test on new shower liners; the International Residential Code allows a shorter one. For diagnosis at home, the longer you wait, the more certain the result. A dropping water level, with no evaporation to blame, or a new stain below means the pan or drain is leaking.

## The shower valve: a different kind of leak

A **leaking shower valve or supply pipe behind the wall** acts differently:

- It leaks when the shower is **on**, or **all the time** if the supply line itself leaks
- Water shows up in the **wall** behind the valve, or on the other side of that wall
- A meter test with everything off catches a constant supply leak

See [how to find a water leak inside a wall](/guides/how-to-find-a-leak-in-a-wall/) for tracing it.

## Repair options

| Problem | Typical fix |
|---|---|
| Leak at the drain connection | Reseal or replace the drain assembly; sometimes possible from below |
| Grout or tile letting water through | Regrout and seal, if the liner underneath is intact |
| Cracked acrylic or fiberglass base | Replace the base |
| Failed liner under a tile floor | Remove the tile floor, replace the liner, retile |
| Valve or supply leak | Repair or replace the valve through an access panel or small wall opening |

Before any demolition, the wet area should be mapped. Subfloor and framing under a long-leaking shower can be soaked well beyond the shower itself.

A burst pipe can put hundreds of gallons of water into a house in an hour, and in central Indiana winters it's one of the most common homeowner claims. What you do in the first few hours affects both the damage and the claim. Here's a step-by-step guide for Indiana homeowners, from shutting off the water to talking to the adjuster.

## Right now: stop the damage

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** Note the time.
2. **Turn off power** to wet areas at the breaker, only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot.
3. **Turn off the water heater** (breaker for electric, vacation or off for gas) if the water will be off for a while.
4. **Open faucets** to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
5. **Move belongings** out of the water.

The full first-hour checklist is in [water leak emergency: what to do first](/guides/water-leak-emergency-what-to-do/).

## Document everything before cleanup

- **Photos and video** of the water, the damage, the broken pipe if visible, and every damaged item
- **Wide shots and close-ups**, with a sense of scale
- **The date and time** you found the leak and shut off the water
- **Damaged belongings:** keep them if you can, or photograph them with brand, model and age
- **The cause:** a photo of the split pipe, the frozen section, or the failed fitting

## Call your insurer or agent the same day

Report the loss promptly and ask:

- Is this covered, and what's my deductible?
- Can I start water removal and drying now? (Usually yes; policies expect you to prevent further damage.)
- Do you want me to use a particular mitigation company, or can I choose?
- When will an adjuster contact me?
- Does my policy include additional living expenses if we can't stay in the house?

Write down the claim number and the name of everyone you talk to.

## What's usually covered, and what isn't

| Usually covered | Often not covered |
|---|---|
| Sudden water damage to walls, ceilings, floors, cabinets | The broken pipe or fitting itself |
| Damaged belongings (personal property coverage) | Damage from a slow leak over weeks or months |
| Water removal and drying | Wear and tear, corrosion, lack of maintenance |
| Tearing out and replacing materials to reach the pipe (often) | Freeze damage in a vacant or unheated home (often limited) |
| Temporary housing if the home is unlivable (if in the policy) | Mold beyond a policy's mold limit |

*Every policy is different. Read yours, especially the water damage, freezing and mold sections.*

## Start drying fast

The U.S. EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within **24–48 hours** to prevent mold growth ([EPA mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold)). Insurers expect reasonable mitigation, so don't wait for the adjuster to start removing water. Save every receipt: pumps, fans, dehumidifier rentals, the water mitigation company, hotel stays.

## When the leak is hidden

Not every burst pipe is obvious. A pipe can split inside a wall, a ceiling or a crawl space, and the only sign is a stain or a moving meter. Guessing where to open walls adds cost and delay. A [thermal imaging inspection](/services/thermal-imaging-inspection/) and [hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) help your claim by:

- **Locating the leak** so only the right area is opened
- **Mapping how far moisture has spread** behind walls and under floors, with thermal images and moisture readings
- **Documenting the cause** and the date, in a photo report you can give your adjuster

If a pipe froze, see [frozen pipe leaks](/guides/frozen-pipe-leaks/) for where to check after a thaw; a frozen pipe often leaks in more than one place.

## Working with the adjuster

- Walk the adjuster through the damage and give them your photos and any leak detection report.
- Ask for the estimate in writing, line by line.
- Compare it with your contractor's estimate, and ask about anything missing, such as matching flooring or drying costs.
- Don't sign a final release until you're satisfied the repairs are covered.

## If you disagree with the decision

Ask the insurer to explain the denial or low offer in writing, citing the policy language. If talking to the company doesn't resolve it, the **Indiana Department of Insurance** takes consumer complaints through its online portal or its consumer hotline, 1-800-622-4461 ([IDOI complaints](https://www.in.gov/idoi/consumer-services/complaints/)). Insurers must respond to the Department in writing.

For slab leaks specifically, see [does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks](/guides/does-insurance-cover-slab-leaks/).

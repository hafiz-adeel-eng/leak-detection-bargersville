A steady hiss or trickle when every faucet in the house is off is worth taking seriously. Sometimes it's harmless, like a toilet refilling. But it's also one of the most common ways people first notice a hidden leak in a wall or under a slab, long before a stain or a big bill. Here's how to work out what you're hearing in about 10 minutes.

## Common causes of a running water sound

| Cause | What it sounds like | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Toilet leaking into the bowl** | Faint trickle, or the tank refilling by itself every so often | Dye test (below) |
| **Hidden supply line leak** | Steady hiss that doesn't stop, often louder at night | Meter test |
| **Slab leak** | Running water or hissing heard through the floor | Meter test, then shutoff and water heater tests |
| **Water softener or water heater cycling** | Runs for a while, then stops | Check whether the unit is in a regeneration or heating cycle |
| **Irrigation valve not fully closing** | Water sound near the main line or outside wall | Close the irrigation shutoff and listen again |
| **High water pressure** | Noise and banging in the pipes | Pressure gauge on an outside faucet |

## Step 1: The 10-minute meter test

1. Turn off every faucet, shower, washer, dishwasher and ice maker. Make sure nobody flushes.
2. Find your water meter and look at the **leak indicator**, usually a small triangle, star or dial.
3. Watch it for 10 minutes, or note the reading and check again after an hour or two.
4. **If it moves, water is leaving the system.** If it's perfectly still, the sound is probably not a supply line leak.

The EPA's WaterSense program estimates that household leaks can waste nearly **10,000 gallons a year** in an average home, and that 1 in 10 homes has leaks wasting **90 gallons or more a day** ([EPA WaterSense Fix a Leak Week](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week)). A running sound you can hear usually means a leak at the larger end of that range.

## Step 2: Rule out the toilets

Toilets are the most common cause of a running sound, and the cheapest to fix.

1. Put a few drops of food coloring in each toilet tank.
2. Wait 15 minutes without flushing.
3. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking.

Replacing a worn flapper usually stops it. Then repeat the meter test; if the meter still moves, something else is leaking too.

## Step 3: Narrow down where the sound is coming from

- **Close the main shutoff in the house.** If the meter still moves, the leak is in the buried line between the meter and the house. See [how to find an underground water leak](/guides/how-to-find-an-underground-water-leak/).
- **Close the cold water inlet on the water heater.** If the meter stops, the leak is on the **hot** side, which in a slab home often means a hot water line under the floor.
- **Close the irrigation shutoff** if you have sprinklers, and check again.
- **Listen at the quietest time of day.** Put your ear to the floor, a wall or a closet near the plumbing. The sound is usually loudest closest to the leak.
- **Feel the floor.** A warm spot on tile or wood over a running sound is a strong sign of a hot water [slab leak](/guides/how-to-find-a-slab-leak/).

## Why you can hear a leak you can't see

Water escaping from a small hole in a pressurized pipe makes noise: a hiss from the pipe itself and a rushing sound as it hits soil, concrete or the wall cavity. In a slab home, that noise travels through the concrete, which is why many homeowners first notice a slab leak as a running sound in a quiet room at night.

That same sound is what professionals use to find it.

## How a leak you can hear gets pinpointed

Professional [slab leak detection](/services/slab-leak-detection/) turns the sound into an exact spot:

- **Isolation and pressure testing** confirm which line is leaking: hot, cold or the service line.
- **Line tracing** maps where that pipe runs under the floor or in the wall.
- **Acoustic ground microphones** are moved along the line; the point where the sound is loudest marks the leak.
- **Thermal imaging** confirms a hot water leak by the warm bloom on the floor.
- **Tracer gas** finds quiet leaks in plastic pipe that don't make much noise.

The spot is marked so the plumber makes one small opening. More detail on each tool is in [how professional leak detection works](/guides/leak-detection-methods/).

## When to call

Call if the meter moves after you've ruled out the toilets, if you hear running water under the floor, if a floor feels warm, or if your [water bill has jumped](/guides/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high/). A hidden leak running around the clock wastes water, can raise your energy bill if it's on the hot side, and can soak framing, flooring and soil under the slab.

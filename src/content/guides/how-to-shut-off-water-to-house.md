When a pipe bursts or a hidden leak is flooding a room, the single most useful thing you can do is shut off the water, fast. Most homeowners don't know where their main shutoff is until they need it. Here's how to find it, how to close it, and what to do if it won't budge.

## Where to find the main shutoff

The main shutoff is almost always where the water line **enters the house**:

| Home type | Where to look |
|---|---|
| **Basement** | On the wall facing the street, low on the wall or near the floor, often right after the meter |
| **Crawl space** | Near the crawl space entry or where the line comes through the foundation |
| **Slab home** | Near the water heater, in a utility closet, or in the garage wall |
| **Water meter inside** | Right beside the meter; there's often a valve on each side |
| **Private well** | Near the pressure tank, on the line going into the house |

Look for a round **gate-valve** handle or a **lever** handle on a pipe about ¾ to 1 inch in diameter.

## How to close it

- **Lever (ball) valve:** turn the handle a **quarter turn** so it's **across** the pipe. Open is in line with the pipe.
- **Round (gate) valve:** turn **clockwise** until it stops. It may take several turns.

Then **open a faucet** on the lowest floor to drain pressure and confirm the water is off.

## If the valve is stuck or won't close

Old gate valves are notorious for seizing, or for breaking inside so the water won't fully stop.

- Use **gentle back-and-forth pressure**, not a long wrench.
- If it won't close, go to the **meter pit** or **curb stop** (below).
- If it closes but water still trickles, the gate may be worn. Have a plumber replace it with a **ball valve** once the emergency is over.

## Outside: the meter pit and curb stop

In many Indiana homes the water meter sits in a **meter pit** in the yard near the street, under a round lid. There's usually a valve on the utility side of the meter, and a **curb stop** near the property line.

- These belong to the **utility**. Call your water utility's emergency number first if you can.
- In a true emergency, many utilities allow a homeowner to close the meter-pit valve with a meter key or channel-lock pliers. Some don't. Know your utility's rule before you need it.
- Don't dig for or force a curb stop yourself.

## Shut off individual fixtures

You don't always need to shut off the whole house. Most fixtures have their own valve:

- **Toilets:** oval handle on the wall behind the toilet
- **Sinks:** two valves under the sink
- **Washing machine:** hot and cold valves behind the washer
- **Water heater:** cold inlet valve on top of the tank
- **Refrigerator:** a small valve behind the fridge, under the sink, or in the basement
- **Irrigation:** a separate valve, often in the basement or near the meter

## Before you need it: a 5-minute drill

1. **Find the main shutoff** and tag it so anyone in the house can find it.
2. **Test it** once a year: close it, check a faucet, open it again.
3. **Replace an old gate valve** with a quarter-turn ball valve.
4. **Show everyone** in the house where it is.
5. **Save your utility's emergency number** in your phone.

## After the water is off

- **Turn off power** to wet areas if it's safe.
- **Turn off the water heater** if the water will be off a while.
- **Take photos** for insurance.
- **Find the source.** A visible [burst pipe](/guides/burst-pipe-what-to-do/) needs a plumber; a hidden leak needs to be found first.

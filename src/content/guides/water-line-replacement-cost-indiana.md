When the buried line between your water meter and your house starts leaking, the first question is almost always *how much?* The honest answer is that the price swings a lot, and the biggest factor is whether you need a **repair** or a full **replacement**. Here's what drives the cost in central Indiana, typical ranges, and how to avoid paying to replace a line that only needed one fix.

## Typical costs

National cost guides give these ballpark ranges for a residential main water line (the "service line"):

| Job | Typical range |
|---|---|
| **Full replacement, trenched** | about $2,000–$5,800 total, or roughly $50–$290 per foot |
| **Full replacement, trenchless** | about $3,200–$8,000 total, or roughly $70–$250 per foot |
| **Spot repair of one leak** | usually a fraction of replacement, depending on depth and access |

*Ranges are from national cost guides such as [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/what-general-cost-water-main-line-replacement.htm) and [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/water-line-replacement-cost). Local prices in Johnson County vary; get written quotes.*

## What drives the price

- **Length of the line.** A house set far back from the street, common on larger lots around Trafalgar and rural Johnson County, simply has more pipe.
- **Depth.** Lines in central Indiana are buried below the frost line, usually 36 inches or deeper, so digging is real work.
- **What's on top.** Lawn is cheap to restore. Driveways, sidewalks, patios, mature trees and irrigation lines are not.
- **Pipe material.** Copper, PEX and HDPE each cost differently. Old galvanized or lead lines usually mean replacement, not repair.
- **Access at the house.** Where the line comes through the foundation or into a basement or crawl space affects labor.
- **Permits and utility coordination.** Towns and utilities may require a permit, an inspection, or a utility visit at the meter.
- **Utility locates.** Before anyone digs, Indiana 811 must be called to mark buried utilities. It's free, and it's the law.

## Repair or replace?

| Situation | Usually |
|---|---|
| One leak, line otherwise sound, reasonable age | **Spot repair** |
| Galvanized steel or lead line | **Replace** |
| Repeated leaks in the last few years | **Replace** |
| Line crushed by roots or settlement in several places | **Replace** |
| Low pressure because the old line has narrowed inside | **Replace** |
| Leak under a driveway, line otherwise good | Repair, or a short **reroute** around the driveway |

The expensive mistake is replacing a whole line because nobody knew where the single leak was, or digging three holes to find it. That's what [underground water leak detection](/services/underground-water-leak-detection/) prevents: the leak is located and marked first, so the plumber can quote an accurate repair.

## Who pays: you or the utility?

In most Indiana communities, the **utility** owns the meter and the pipe up to it, and the **homeowner** owns the service line from the meter to the house. If the leak is right at the meter or on the street side, call your utility first. Johnson County homes are served by several providers, including Indiana American Water, the Town of Bargersville water utility and town utilities such as Whiteland and Trafalgar. See our [town pages](/service-area/) for who serves your area.

Lead service lines are a special case. Indiana American Water has state approval to replace customer-owned lead service lines at no direct charge when it finds them during its own lead line replacement work ([Indiana American Water lead service FAQs](https://amwater.com/inaw/water-quality/lead-and-drinking-water/lead-service-faqs)). If you think your line is lead, ask your utility before paying for a replacement.

## How to keep the cost down

1. **Confirm it's the service line.** Turn off all water, then close the main shutoff in the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is outside. See [how to find an underground water leak](/guides/how-to-find-an-underground-water-leak/).
2. **Pinpoint the leak before anyone digs.** Line location, acoustic listening and tracer gas find the spot so only one small hole is dug.
3. **Ask about a spot repair** when the line is in otherwise good condition.
4. **Get two or three written quotes** that state the pipe material, depth, restoration of lawn or concrete, and permit costs.
5. **Ask your utility about a leak adjustment** on the high bill once the repair is done. See [water bill leak adjustments](/guides/water-bill-leak-adjustment-indiana/).

## Signs your service line is leaking

- A soggy, sunken or greener strip between the meter and the house
- Water pooling near the meter or at the foundation
- [Low water pressure](/guides/low-water-pressure-leak/) throughout the house
- A meter that moves with everything off, and a higher bill

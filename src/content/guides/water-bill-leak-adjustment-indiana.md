A leak can double or triple a water bill before anyone notices. The good news is that many utilities offer a **leak adjustment**, a partial credit for water lost through a leak on your side of the meter, once the leak is fixed. The catch is that every utility sets its own rules. Here's how leak adjustments usually work in Indiana, what to have ready, and how to ask the utilities that serve Johnson County.

## How leak adjustments usually work

Policies vary, but most share these features:

- **The leak must be on your side of the meter:** the service line, the plumbing in the house, or irrigation.
- **It must be repaired** before the credit is given.
- **You need proof:** a plumber's invoice or receipts, the repair date, sometimes photos.
- **There's a deadline,** often a set number of days after the bill or the repair.
- **Limits apply:** often one adjustment in a 12-month period, and a credit for only part of the extra water.
- **Sewer charges** may be adjusted separately if the leaked water never went down the drain.
- **Some losses are excluded,** such as water used on purpose (filling a pool, watering), or leaks that were ignored after the customer knew about them.

Some utilities have a written policy or a leak adjustment form; others decide case by case. Either way, you have to ask.

## Step 1: Confirm and fix the leak fast

The faster the leak is found and fixed, the smaller the bill and the stronger your request.

1. **Meter test:** turn off all water and watch the meter's leak indicator. If it moves, water is leaking. See [how to find a water leak in your house](/guides/how-to-find-a-water-leak-in-your-house/).
2. **Rule out the easy ones:** a running toilet (dye test) or a dripping outdoor faucet. Walk through [why your water bill is so high](/guides/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high/).
3. **If it's hidden,** under a slab, in a wall or in the buried line, get it located so the repair happens right away rather than after weeks of guessing.

## Step 2: Gather your proof

- **The high bill(s)** and one or two normal bills for comparison
- **Meter readings** before and after the repair, with dates. A photo of the meter works.
- **The repair invoice** from the plumber, or receipts for parts if you fixed it yourself
- **Photos** of the leak and the repair
- **A leak detection report**, if you had one, showing where the leak was, what line it was on, and the date it was found

A report that shows the leak was hidden, such as a slab leak or a buried line, also helps show you couldn't have seen it sooner.

## Step 3: Contact your utility

Call customer service, say you had a leak on your side of the meter that's now repaired, and ask:

- Do you offer a leak adjustment, and is there a form?
- What's the deadline?
- What documents do you need?
- Does it cover sewer charges as well as water?
- How is the credit calculated?

## If the answer is no

- **Ask what would change the decision,** such as a sewer-only credit or a payment plan.
- **Ask for a supervisor or a written decision.**
- For utilities regulated by the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission, such as Indiana American Water, the **Indiana Office of Utility Consumer Counselor** represents consumers and can explain your options ([OUCC](https://www.in.gov/oucc/)).

## Avoid the next surprise bill

- Check the meter's leak indicator once a month with everything off.
- Fix running toilets right away. They're one of the most common causes of a high bill.
- Consider a [smart water leak detector or flow monitor](/guides/smart-water-leak-detectors/) that alerts you to continuous flow.
- Get hidden leaks found early. A leak that runs around the clock can waste thousands of gallons a month.

A sprinkler leak can waste more water than every faucet in the house combined, and because the pipe is buried, it often goes unnoticed until the summer water bill arrives. Here's how to track one down in your own yard, zone by zone.

## Signs your irrigation system is leaking

- **Soggy, spongy or sunken spots** in the lawn, especially along a straight line
- **Patches that stay greener** than the rest of the yard
- **Water bubbling or pooling** near a sprinkler head or the valve box
- **One zone with weak or uneven spray**, or heads that don't pop up fully
- **The water meter moving** while the sprinklers are off
- **A jump in the water bill** during watering season

## Mainline or lateral line? The one test that matters

Before you dig, find out *which part* of the system is leaking. Most residential systems have two kinds of pipe:

| Pipe | Pressurized when? | What a leak looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Mainline** (backflow preventer → zone valves) | All the time | Meter moves even with the sprinklers off; wet spot near the valve box or along the main run |
| **Lateral lines** (valve → heads) | Only while that zone runs | Soggy spot or weak pressure in one zone, only during or after watering |

**The test:**

1. Make sure the sprinklers and all indoor water are off.
2. Check the water meter for 15–30 minutes.
3. Close the **irrigation isolation valve**, usually near the backflow preventer or where the irrigation tees off the main water line.
4. If the meter **stops**, the leak is in the irrigation mainline or a valve. If it **keeps moving**, the leak is in the house or the main service line. See [how to find an underground water leak](/guides/how-to-find-an-underground-water-leak/).

## How to find the leak zone by zone

1. **Turn on one zone manually** from the controller.
2. **Walk the zone** and watch each head. Look for geysers, heads that barely rise, water seeping around a head's base, and wet soil between heads.
3. **Listen** near the valve box. A hissing valve that doesn't fully close points to a worn diaphragm or debris in the valve.
4. **Let it run for 10–15 minutes**, then walk it again. Slow leaks often show up as a spreading wet patch after a few minutes.
5. **Repeat for every zone** and note which one has the problem.

> **Tip:** Mark suspicious spots with a flag or a screwdriver as you go. After all the zones have run, the pattern usually points to one pipe run.

## Check the valve box and backflow preventer

Many irrigation leaks aren't in the pipe at all:

- **Valve box full of water:** a leaking valve body, a loose fitting, or a cracked solenoid seal.
- **Weeping valve:** a zone that keeps dribbling after it shuts off usually has a worn diaphragm.
- **Backflow preventer dripping or split:** common after a freeze if the system wasn't drained completely.

## Why spring start-up is when most leaks show up

In central Indiana, sprinkler systems are usually winterized each fall by blowing the lines out with compressed air. Any water left in a low spot, fitting or backflow preventer can freeze, expand and crack the part. Those cracks stay hidden until the system is pressurized again in spring. That's why the first run of the season is the best time to walk every zone.

## When a sprinkler leak needs professional detection

DIY checks find most broken heads and obvious wet spots. Call a professional when:

- the meter shows a mainline leak but there's no wet spot,
- the leak is under a driveway, patio or landscaping you don't want to dig up,
- the soil drains so well that water never surfaces, or
- you can't tell whether it's the sprinkler line or the [main water line to the house](/services/underground-water-leak-detection/).

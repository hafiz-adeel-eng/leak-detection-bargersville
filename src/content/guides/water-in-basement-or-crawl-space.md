Water in a basement or crawl space has three usual sources: a **plumbing leak**, **groundwater or rainwater** coming in from outside, or **condensation** from humid air. They need completely different fixes. A plumber can't waterproof a foundation, and a waterproofing company can't stop a leaking pipe. A few simple tests tell you which one you're dealing with before you pay anyone.

## Three sources, three fixes

| Source | Typical clues | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Plumbing leak** | Meter moves with water off; wet near pipes, the water heater or washer; water appears in dry weather | Leak detection, then a plumber |
| **Groundwater or rain** | Appears during or after rain or snowmelt; wet along the wall–floor joint or at cracks; efflorescence (white crust) on walls | Grading, gutters, drainage or waterproofing |
| **Condensation** | Sweating pipes and walls in humid summer weather; damp all over rather than one spot | Dehumidifier, insulation on cold pipes, ventilation |

## Test 1: The meter test

This is the quickest way to rule a plumbing leak in or out.

1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the irrigation system.
2. Watch the leak indicator on your water meter for 15–30 minutes, or compare two readings an hour or two apart.
3. **If it moves, a pressurized pipe is leaking** somewhere, maybe in the basement or crawl space, maybe elsewhere.
4. Close the main shutoff in the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is in the buried service line outside, which can also push water toward the foundation.

A still meter doesn't clear drains: a leaking drain, washer hose or water heater only drips when it's being used, so also check around them after they run.

## Test 2: When does the water appear?

- **Only during or after rain:** outside water. Check gutters and downspouts, and make sure the soil slopes away from the house.
- **In dry weather, in one spot:** plumbing is the prime suspect, especially near pipes, the water heater, washer or a floor drain.
- **In hot, humid weather, in many places:** condensation.
- **After a long wet spell, from the floor or wall joint:** a high water table pushing groundwater up. Central Indiana's clay-rich soil drains slowly and holds water against foundations.

## Test 3: The foil test

For damp walls or floors, tape a 12-inch square of aluminum foil tightly to the surface, sealing all four edges. Leave it 24–48 hours, then peel it off:

- **Wet on the room side:** condensation from humid air.
- **Wet on the wall side:** moisture coming through the concrete from outside or from a leak behind it.

## Test 4: Follow the water

- **Look up.** Water from a pipe often runs along a joist, beam or duct before it drips, so the puddle may be several feet from the leak.
- **Check the usual suspects:** the water heater and its fittings, washer hoses, the laundry sink, the pressure tank on a well system, the main line where it enters, and any outside faucet lines.
- **Check the sump pit.** A pump that runs when it hasn't rained can mean groundwater, or a leak feeding the pit.
- **In a crawl space,** look for wet insulation, puddles under pipes, sagging vapor barrier holding water, and a musty smell. Cold pipes in crawl spaces are also the first to crack in a hard freeze; see [frozen pipe leaks](/guides/frozen-pipe-leaks/).

## Why it matters

The U.S. EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within **24–48 hours** and keeping indoor humidity below **60%**, ideally **30–50%**, to limit mold ([EPA mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold)). A slow plumbing leak in a crawl space can soak soil, insulation and floor framing for weeks, and moisture there travels up into the house.

## When the leak is hidden

If the meter moves but you can't see water, or you've found wet soil or insulation but not the pipe, professional [hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) narrows it down:

- **Isolation tests** show whether the leak is on the hot side, cold side or service line.
- **Thermal imaging** shows wet areas on walls, floors and framing, and the warm trail of a hot water leak.
- **Moisture mapping** confirms how far the water has spread.
- **Acoustic listening** finds pressurized leaks in walls, under slabs and along buried lines.

A [thermal imaging inspection](/services/thermal-imaging-inspection/) with a photo report also documents the moisture for insurance, or before you buy an older home.

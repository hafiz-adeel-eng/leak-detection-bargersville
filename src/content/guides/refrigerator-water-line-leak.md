Refrigerator water lines are small, out of sight and under constant pressure, and they're one of the most common sources of hidden kitchen leaks. A slow drip behind the fridge can soak the subfloor for weeks, warp flooring, and show up as a stain on the basement ceiling before anyone pulls the fridge out. Here's how to find the leak, fix it properly, and check how far the water went.

## Signs of a refrigerator line leak

- **Water on the floor** under or beside the fridge, especially toward the back
- **Warped, cupped or stained flooring** near the fridge
- **Loose tile** or dark grout in front of it
- A **stain on the ceiling** below the kitchen, or water in the basement or crawl space
- A **musty smell** in the kitchen
- An ice maker that makes **less ice**, or a slow water dispenser
- A **water meter that moves** with everything off

## Step 1: Make it safe and dry

1. **Turn off the water** to the fridge at its shutoff valve, usually behind the fridge, under the sink, or in the basement below.
2. **Unplug the fridge** before moving it or reaching behind it.
3. **Pull it out carefully** so you don't crush or kink the line.
4. **Dry everything:** the floor, the valve, the line and the fittings.

## Step 2: Find the leak

Turn the water back on and watch each spot with a dry paper towel:

| Where | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| **Shutoff valve** | A **saddle valve** clamped onto a pipe is the most common slow leak; old valves weep at the stem |
| **Fitting at the valve** | Loose or overtightened compression nut |
| **The line itself** | Cracked, brittle or kinked **plastic tubing**; pinholes in old copper |
| **Fitting at the fridge** | Loose connection at the inlet valve |
| **Inside the fridge** | Failed inlet valve, cracked fill tube, or a clogged defrost drain that overflows onto the floor |

Water that's only there after the defrost cycle, or that appears inside the fridge, is usually a drain or ice maker problem, not the supply line.

## Step 3: Fix it properly

- **Replace a saddle valve** with a proper tee and **quarter-turn shutoff valve**. Saddle valves are one of the most common sources of slow, hard-to-notice ice maker leaks.
- **Replace plastic tubing** with a **braided stainless steel** line with factory-crimped fittings.
- **Leave a loose loop** of slack so the fridge can be pulled out without kinking the line.
- **Don't overtighten** compression fittings. Snug plus a quarter turn is usually enough.
- **Check it again** after a day, and after you push the fridge back.

## Step 4: Check for hidden damage

A refrigerator leak often runs **under the flooring** and **toward the lowest point**, which can be several feet from the fridge. Check:

- The **subfloor**, if you can see it from a basement or crawl space
- The **ceiling** below the kitchen
- **Cabinets** next to the fridge, including the toe-kick space
- The **floor in front** of the fridge for soft or hollow spots

## Prevent the next one

- Check behind the fridge **once a year**, or when you clean the coils.
- Put a **leak sensor** on the floor behind the fridge. See [smart water leak detectors](/guides/smart-water-leak-detectors/).
- Know where the fridge's **shutoff valve** is, and make sure it turns.

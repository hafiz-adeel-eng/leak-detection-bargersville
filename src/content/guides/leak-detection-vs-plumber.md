When water is going somewhere it shouldn't, most people call a plumber first. Sometimes that's exactly right. But for leaks you can't see, finding the leak and fixing it are two different jobs, and it helps to know who does which.

## What a plumber does

A plumber installs, repairs and replaces pipes, fixtures and water heaters. When the leak is **visible**, a plumber is the right call:

- a dripping faucet or showerhead
- a running toilet
- a leaking supply line or drain under a sink
- a failing water heater
- a burst pipe you can see

## What a leak detection specialist does

A leak detection specialist **finds hidden leaks without opening things up to search**. The work is testing and pinpointing:

- meter and isolation tests to confirm the leak and find the line
- pressure testing each line
- acoustic ground microphones, correlators and tracer gas to pinpoint the spot
- thermal imaging and moisture mapping inside walls and floors
- marking the location and documenting it with photos

The output is a **marked spot**, not a repaired pipe. See [how professional leak detection works](/guides/leak-detection-methods/).

## Why the order matters

Without detection, the only way to find a hidden leak is to open things up: break tile, cut drywall or dig a trench, and keep going until the water turns up. Every wrong opening is more concrete, flooring or landscaping to replace.

With detection first:

1. The leak is **confirmed** and the leaking line is **isolated**.
2. The exact spot is **marked**.
3. The plumber opens **one small area** and repairs the pipe.

## Can one company do both?

Some can. Either way, ask the same question: *how will you pinpoint the leak before you open anything?* If the answer is a clear testing process, you're in good hands.

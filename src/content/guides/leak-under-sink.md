A wet sink cabinet is one of the most common leaks in any home, and usually one of the easiest to fix, once you know which of the half-dozen connections under there is the culprit. Here's how to track it down in a kitchen or bathroom, and what to do when the leak is actually in the wall behind the cabinet.

## Before you start

1. **Empty the cabinet.**
2. **Dry everything:** pipes, valves, the cabinet floor.
3. **Wrap connections** in dry paper towel or tissue so drips show up clearly.
4. Have a **flashlight** and a bucket ready.

## Test one thing at a time

| Test | If it leaks | Likely source |
|---|---|---|
| **Faucet off**, wait 10 minutes | Water anyway | **Supply lines** or **shutoff valves** |
| **Faucet running**, stopper out | Water while running | **Faucet body**, sprayer hose, base seal |
| **Fill the sink, then drain** | Water while draining | **Drain**, **P-trap**, basket strainer, tailpiece |
| **Run the disposal** | Water from the disposal | **Disposal** seals or connections |
| **Run the dishwasher** | Water during the cycle | **Dishwasher** drain or supply connection |

## Common causes and fixes

- **Supply lines:** replace old plastic or rubber lines with **braided stainless** lines. Tighten, but don't overtighten.
- **Shutoff valves:** a valve that weeps at the stem can often be snugged; an old multi-turn valve is better replaced with a quarter-turn valve.
- **P-trap and drain:** hand-tighten slip nuts, replace worn washers, and make sure the pieces are aligned.
- **Basket strainer** (kitchen): a leak around the drain opening usually needs the strainer reset with fresh plumber's putty.
- **Faucet:** leaks from the base or under the deck are often a worn O-ring or cartridge, or a loose sprayer hose.
- **Garbage disposal:** leaks at the top are the sink flange; at the side, the drain or dishwasher connection; from the bottom, often an internal seal, which usually means replacing the disposal.
- **Dishwasher:** check the drain hose clamp at the disposal or tailpiece, and the supply connection under the sink.

Bathroom sinks add the **pop-up drain** assembly, which leaks at the pivot nut.

## When the leak is in the wall

If the cabinet floor or back wall is wet but every connection stays dry:

- The **supply stubs** or **drain line** inside the wall may be leaking.
- A **refrigerator water line** or **dishwasher line** running through the cabinet may be leaking. See [refrigerator water line leak](/guides/refrigerator-water-line-leak/).
- Water from a **window** or exterior wall above the sink may be running down inside the wall.

Do a **meter test** with everything off. If the meter moves, a pressurized line is leaking somewhere. A moisture meter on the back wall of the cabinet shows whether the wall is wet. See [how to find a water leak inside a wall](/guides/how-to-find-a-leak-in-a-wall/).

## Check for damage

- **Swollen or soft cabinet floor** (particleboard swells when wet)
- **Mold** on the back of the cabinet or wall
- **Stains** on the ceiling below, if the sink is upstairs
- **Flooring damage** in front of the cabinet

A long-running leak under a sink can soak the subfloor. [Hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) with moisture mapping shows how far it went, so only damaged material is replaced.

## Prevent it

- Replace supply lines with **braided** lines and check them once a year.
- Put a **leak sensor** in the sink cabinet.
- Don't store heavy items that can knock the P-trap loose.

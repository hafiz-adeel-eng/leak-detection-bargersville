Repiping means replacing the water supply lines throughout the house. It's a big job, but for a home with old, failing pipe it's often cheaper in the long run than paying for leak after leak and the water damage that comes with them. Here's what it costs, PEX versus copper, and the signs it's time.

## Typical costs

| | Typical range (national) |
|---|---|
| **Whole-house repipe, overall** | about $4,500–$15,000 |
| **PEX repipe** | about $4,000–$9,000 |
| **Copper repipe** | about $8,000–$15,000+ |
| **Drywall and paint repair afterward** | often $1,500–$3,000 extra |

*Ranges are from national cost guides such as [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-to-repipe-a-house.htm). Prices in Johnson County vary; get written quotes.*

## What drives the price

- **Home size and layout:** more pipe and more fixtures cost more.
- **Number of bathrooms and fixtures.**
- **Pipe material:** PEX costs less in material and labor than copper.
- **Access:** an unfinished basement or crawl space makes it easier; a slab home with finished ceilings means more wall openings.
- **Stories:** two-story homes need vertical runs through walls.
- **Drywall repair:** some plumbers include patching; many don't.
- **Permits and inspection.**

## PEX vs copper

| | PEX | Copper |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Lower | Higher |
| **Install** | Flexible, fewer fittings, fewer wall openings | Rigid, more fittings and soldered joints |
| **Freeze resistance** | Better; can expand somewhat | Can split when frozen |
| **Pinholes** | Not subject to copper pitting | Can pinhole in some water conditions |
| **Sunlight** | Must be protected from UV | Not affected |
| **Track record** | Decades of use | Over a century of use |

Most repipes today use PEX because of cost and speed. Both are code-approved in most areas.

## Signs it's time to repipe

- **Several leaks in a few years**, especially [pinhole leaks in copper](/guides/pinhole-leaks-copper-pipes/)
- **Galvanized steel pipe**, which corrodes inside and restricts flow. See [galvanized pipe replacement](/guides/galvanized-pipe-replacement/).
- **Polybutylene pipe**, gray plastic pipe from the late 1970s to mid-1990s. See [polybutylene pipe replacement](/guides/polybutylene-pipe-replacement/).
- **Rusty or discolored water**, low pressure throughout the house
- **Repeated slab leaks**. Rerouting overhead often replaces the worst of it. See [slab leak repair cost](/guides/slab-leak-repair-cost/).

## Repipe or reroute?

A **reroute** replaces one line, typically a leaking line under the slab, with a new line run through walls or the attic. A **repipe** replaces the whole supply system. If only one line is failing and the rest of the pipe is sound, rerouting may be enough. A plumber can inspect the exposed pipe to judge.

## How leak detection fits in

Before committing to a repipe, it helps to know:

- **Where the current leak is,** so the damage can be dried and the repair or reroute planned
- **Which line** is failing (hot, cold or service line)
- **How widespread the moisture is** in walls and floors

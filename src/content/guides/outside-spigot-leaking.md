Outdoor spigots, also called hose bibs or sillcocks, are one of the most common sources of leaks in Indiana homes, and one of the easiest to overlook. Some leaks are just an annoying drip outside. Others happen inside the wall, where they soak framing, insulation and the basement ceiling every time the hose runs. Here's how to tell which one you have.

## What kind of leak is it?

| Where the water is | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Drips from the spout** when off | Worn washer or cartridge | Replace the washer or cartridge |
| **Around the handle** when on | Loose packing nut or worn packing | Tighten the nut a quarter turn; repack |
| **From the vacuum breaker** on top | Worn vacuum breaker, or normal brief drain | Replace the vacuum breaker if it keeps leaking |
| **Inside the house or down the foundation** when on | **Cracked valve body or pipe** behind the wall | Replace the spigot; check the wall |
| **At the hose connection** | Worn hose washer | New hose washer |

## The leak you can't see: frost-free spigots

A **frost-free spigot** has a long valve body that reaches through the wall, so the actual shutoff sits inside the heated house. When you turn it off, the water in the outside section drains out, so there's nothing left to freeze.

That only works if the water can drain. **Leave a hose connected**, and water stays trapped in the long valve body. In a hard freeze it cracks, **inside the wall**. You won't know until spring, when you turn the spigot on and water runs into the wall cavity, the rim joist or the basement instead of out the spout.

Signs of a cracked frost-free spigot:

- **Weak flow** from the spout when it's fully open
- **Water in the basement** or crawl space under the spigot, or on the basement ceiling
- **Stains or damp drywall** on the inside wall behind the spigot
- **Water running down the foundation** below the spigot outside

## How to test it

1. Turn the spigot **fully on** for a minute or two with no hose attached.
2. Check **inside**: the basement ceiling, the rim joist, the wall behind the spigot, the crawl space.
3. If water appears inside, **close the spigot** and the shutoff valve for that line, if it has one.

If the spigot doesn't have its own inside shutoff, you may need to shut off the main until it's fixed. See [how to shut off the water to your house](/guides/how-to-shut-off-water-to-house/).

## Fixing it

- **Dripping or handle leaks:** replace the washer, cartridge or packing. These are simple repairs.
- **Cracked frost-free spigot:** the whole spigot is replaced, from outside or inside depending on how it was installed. Check that the new one **slopes slightly downward** toward the outside so it drains.
- **Cracked pipe behind the spigot:** the damaged section is cut out and replaced along with the spigot.

## Check for hidden water damage

A cracked spigot often runs into the wall every time the hose is used, for weeks. Once it's fixed:

- Check the **rim joist**, **sill plate** and **insulation** from the basement or crawl space.
- Look for **mold** on framing or the back of drywall.
- Check the **interior wall** for soft or stained drywall.

If you can't see into the wall, [hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) with a thermal camera and moisture meter shows how far the water went without opening the whole wall. See also [how to find a water leak inside a wall](/guides/how-to-find-a-leak-in-a-wall/).

## Winterize every fall

In central Indiana, do this before the first hard freeze:

1. **Disconnect every hose**, including hose splitters and quick-connects.
2. **Drain** the hoses and store them.
3. If a spigot **isn't frost-free**, close its inside shutoff and open the spigot outside to drain.
4. Consider **insulated spigot covers**, especially on the north and west sides.

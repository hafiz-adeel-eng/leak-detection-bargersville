A little water around the base of the toilet is easy to mop up and forget. But if it's coming from under the toilet, it's also soaking into the subfloor every time someone flushes, and in a two-story home it can end up in the ceiling below. Here's how to find out where the water is really coming from, what to fix, and how to check for damage you can't see.

## First: where is the water coming from?

Dry the toilet and the floor completely, then watch.

| When it's wet | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Right after flushing, from under the base | **Failed wax ring** or a loose toilet |
| Water beads all over the tank in humid weather | **Condensation** ("sweating" tank) |
| Drip at the wall, under the tank | **Supply line or shutoff valve** |
| Drips from under the tank at the bolts | **Tank bolts or tank-to-bowl gasket** |
| Slow seep even when not flushed | **Crack** in the tank or bowl |

**Tip:** put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If colored water shows up on the floor, the leak is from the tank or bolts. If the water is clear after flushing, look at the wax ring.

## The wax ring: most common cause

The wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange in the floor. It fails when:

- The **toilet rocks**, because the bolts loosened or the floor is uneven, and the rocking breaks the seal
- The ring is **old** and has dried out or compressed
- The toilet was **reset** without a new ring
- The **flange is broken, loose or too low** below the finished floor

Signs of a wax ring leak:

- Water at the base **after flushing**
- A toilet that **rocks** or shifts
- A **sewer smell** in the bathroom
- **Soft floor**, loose tile or stained vinyl around the toilet
- A **stain on the ceiling** below the bathroom

## Fixing it

1. **Tighten the closet bolts** gently if the toilet rocks. Overtightening can crack the porcelain.
2. If water still appears after flushing, **replace the wax ring**: shut off the water, flush and sponge the bowl and tank dry, disconnect the supply, remove the toilet, scrape off the old wax, check the flange, set a new ring and reset the toilet.
3. **Repair or replace a damaged flange** before setting the new ring. A new ring on a broken flange will leak again.
4. **Replace the supply line** if it drips or is old, with a new braided line, and check the shutoff valve.

Don't just run a bead of caulk around a leaking toilet to stop the water showing. That hides the leak; it doesn't fix it.

## Check for hidden damage

A wax ring leak often runs for months before anyone notices, and it's dirty water. Once the toilet is off:

- **Look at the subfloor** around the flange. Dark, soft or crumbling wood needs repair before the toilet goes back.
- **Check the ceiling** below the bathroom for stains, sagging or soft drywall.
- **Check the basement or crawl space** under the bathroom for wet joists or insulation.

## When the leak isn't the toilet

If the floor near the toilet stays wet after you've replaced the wax ring, look at the neighbors: a **shower pan or tub drain** often leaks toward the toilet, and water from a leaking shower can show up at the toilet base. See [shower pan leak](/guides/shower-pan-leak/). A supply line leak inside the wall behind the toilet can also run down to the floor.

And if the toilet makes a running sound or the tank refills by itself, that's a different leak, inside the tank, that wastes water down the drain. See [hear water running when nothing is on](/guides/water-running-sound-in-pipes/).

Driveways often sit right on top of the water service line, and sometimes an irrigation line too. When one of those pipes leaks, the water has nowhere to go but up through cracks and joints, or out at the edge of the concrete. A wet driveway in dry weather is worth checking before it undermines the slab. Here's how to confirm it's a leak and find it without cutting up the driveway to look.

## Signs of a leak under the driveway

- Water **seeping through cracks or control joints** when it hasn't rained
- A **wet or dark patch** that never fully dries, often with moss or algae
- Water trickling out at the **edge of the driveway** or into the street gutter
- A **sunken, cracked or tilting** section of concrete
- A patch of lawn beside the driveway that stays soggy
- A **meter that moves** with everything off, a [higher bill](/guides/why-is-my-water-bill-so-high/) or [lower water pressure](/guides/low-water-pressure-leak/)

## Is it a leak, or just groundwater?

| Clue | Points to |
|---|---|
| Wet in dry weather, day after day | A pressurized **water line** leak |
| Wet only during or after sprinklers run | An **irrigation** line or valve |
| Wet only after rain | **Runoff or groundwater**, a downspout, or drainage |
| Meter moves with all water off | A **leak** on your side of the meter |

## Step 1: Meter test

1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the irrigation system.
2. Watch the meter's leak indicator for 15–30 minutes, or compare readings an hour or two apart.
3. **If it moves, a line is leaking.**

## Step 2: Which line is it?

- **Close the irrigation shutoff.** If the meter stops, the leak is in the sprinkler system. See [how to find a sprinkler leak](/guides/how-to-find-a-sprinkler-leak/).
- **Close the main shutoff in the house.** If the meter keeps moving, the leak is in the **service line** between the meter and the house, which often runs under the driveway.
- **If the meter stops with the house shutoff closed,** the leak is inside the house, and the wet driveway may have another cause.

If the leak looks like it's at the meter or on the street side, call your water utility first. In most Johnson County communities the utility owns the line up to the meter and the homeowner owns the rest.

## Step 3: Pinpoint it before cutting concrete

Concrete is expensive to cut, remove and pour back. Guessing can mean several openings. With [underground water leak detection](/services/underground-water-leak-detection/), we:

- **Trace the line** so you know exactly where it runs under the slab
- **Listen through the concrete** with ground microphones; concrete carries leak noise well, and the loudest point marks the leak
- Use **tracer gas** on plastic lines, which escapes through joints and cracks at the leak
- **Mark the spot** on the driveway for the repair

## Repair options

| Option | When it fits |
|---|---|
| **Cut a small section and repair** | Line is in good shape; one leak |
| **Reroute around the driveway** | Older line, repeated leaks, or you'd rather not cut concrete |
| **Replace the whole service line** | Old galvanized or lead line, several problems. See [water line replacement cost](/guides/water-line-replacement-cost-indiana/) |

After the repair, the base under the concrete should be checked. Water can wash out gravel and soften the clay soil common across central Indiana, and a void left under the slab can lead to cracking later.

## Don't wait

A leak under a driveway runs 24 hours a day, adds to your water bill, and slowly erodes what's holding the concrete up. A freeze makes it worse: water in the base expands, and ice on the driveway surface becomes a slip hazard.

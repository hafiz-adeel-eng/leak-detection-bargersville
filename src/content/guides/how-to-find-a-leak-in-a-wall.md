A leak inside a wall usually comes from a supply line, a drain, or a shower valve that's slowly soaking the drywall and framing. Because the water is hidden behind paint, it can take weeks to show. Here's how to confirm it and narrow down the spot before anyone reaches for a saw.

## Signs of a leak inside a wall

- **Stains or discoloration** on drywall or ceilings, often yellow or brown with a darker edge
- **Bubbling, peeling or cracking paint**
- **Soft, swollen or crumbly drywall**
- **Warped baseboards or trim,** or flooring lifting along the wall
- **A musty smell** in one room or closet
- **Visible mold** on the wall or baseboard
- **The sound of dripping or running water** inside the wall

These signs are most common on walls shared with a **bathroom, kitchen, laundry room or water heater**, because that's where the pipes are.

## Step 1: Is it a supply line or a drain?

The answer changes how you look for it.

| Test | Result | Likely source |
|---|---|---|
| **Meter test:** turn all water off, watch the meter 1–2 hours | Meter moves | A **supply line**. It's under constant pressure, so it leaks all the time. |
| **Run the nearby fixture:** fill and drain the tub, run the shower or sink | Stain grows or the wall gets wetter only after use | A **drain line**, shower valve, or water getting past grout and caulk |
| Neither | Wall still wet | Possibly exterior water intrusion, such as a window, siding or gutter |

## Step 2: Find the wettest point

- **Moisture meter:** an inexpensive pin or pinless meter lets you take readings in a grid across the wall. The highest reading is usually closest to the source.
- **Touch and look:** press gently along the baseboard and the lower wall. Soft spots mark saturated drywall.
- **Check both sides:** look inside the vanity cabinet, behind the toilet and in the room on the other side of the wall.

Remember that water runs **down** and along the framing, so the stain is often *below* the actual leak.

## Step 3: Check the usual suspects

- **Shower valve and supply lines** behind the shower wall
- **Shower pan:** a failed pan leaks when the shower runs, often showing up in the ceiling below
- **Toilet supply line and wax ring**
- **Sink supply lines and drain connections** inside the vanity
- **Washing machine box** in the laundry wall
- **Water heater lines** in a utility closet

## Step 4: Pinpoint before cutting

Cutting drywall in the wrong place means a bigger patch, and with tile walls, broken tile. Professional hidden leak detection narrows it down without opening the wall:

- **Thermal imaging** shows the temperature pattern of a wet or hot area on the wall's surface.
- **Moisture mapping** confirms how far the water has spread.
- **Acoustic listening** can pick up a pressurized supply line leak.
- **Flood testing** checks whether a shower pan holds water.

Once the source is marked, the opening can be small and in the right place. See [how professional leak detection works](/guides/leak-detection-methods/).

## Don't wait on it

The U.S. EPA recommends drying water-damaged materials within **24–48 hours** to prevent mold growth ([EPA mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold)). A hidden leak can keep a wall wet for weeks. If you see signs, turn off the nearest fixture's shutoff valve if you know the source, and get it located.

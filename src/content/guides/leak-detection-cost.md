The honest answer to "how much does leak detection cost?" is: it depends on the leak. A running toilet takes minutes to diagnose. A leak under a concrete slab in a large home takes more time and more equipment. This guide explains what drives the price, so you know what you're paying for and how to compare quotes.

## What you're paying for

Professional leak detection isn't just a visit. The price covers:

- **Diagnosis:** meter and isolation tests to confirm the leak and which line it's on
- **Testing time:** pressure-testing each line that might be leaking
- **Specialized equipment:** acoustic ground microphones, correlators, thermal cameras, tracer gas, moisture meters. See [how professional leak detection works](/guides/leak-detection-methods/).
- **Pinpointing and marking** the leak so the repair starts in the right spot
- **Documentation:** photos and notes for your plumber and insurer

## The factors that change the price

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Where the leak is** | Leaks under a slab or in a buried line take more work than one under a sink. Pools need their own tests. |
| **Number of lines to test** | Hot, cold, main service line, irrigation, pool lines: each one tested adds time. |
| **Pipe material** | Copper carries leak noise well. PEX and PVC are quieter and may need tracer gas. |
| **Access** | Hard-to-reach meters, crawl spaces and heavy landscaping slow the work. |
| **Surface above the pipe** | Thick tile, wood or concrete dampens sound and can take longer to listen through. |
| **Size and layout of the property** | Longer runs mean more ground to cover. |
| **Timing** | Evening, weekend or emergency visits can cost more. |

## Detection cost vs. repair cost

These are two separate costs:

- **Detection** finds and marks the leak.
- **Repair** opens the marked spot and fixes the pipe.

Good detection *lowers* the repair cost, because the plumber opens one small area instead of several. Without it, the "cost" of finding the leak shows up as extra tile, concrete, drywall and landscaping to replace.

## What does slab leak repair cost?

Once a [slab leak is located](/services/slab-leak-detection/), there are three common repair approaches. The right one depends on the pipe's age, the leak's location and whether other leaks are likely.

| Repair approach | What it involves | Cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot repair** | Opening the concrete at the marked spot, repairing the pipe, and patching the slab and flooring | Flooring type, slab thickness, access |
| **Reroute** | Abandoning the leaking section and running a new line through walls or the attic | Length of the run, finished walls and ceilings |
| **Repipe** | Replacing all or most of the home's supply lines | Home size, number of fixtures, pipe material |

A spot repair depends on accurate detection. If the leak isn't pinpointed, the opening gets bigger, and so does the bill. If the pipes are old and leaks keep coming back, rerouting or repiping can make more sense than repeated spot repairs. Your plumber can advise once the leak is found.

## Will insurance help?

Many homeowners policies cover sudden water damage and the cost of accessing the leak, often called "tear out," but not the worn pipe itself. Policies vary, so check yours. A leak detection report with photos and readings makes the claim easier.

## How to keep the total cost down

1. **Act early.** A slow leak costs less to fix before it damages flooring, cabinets or the foundation.
2. **Do a meter test first** so you can describe the problem clearly. See [how to find a water leak in your house](/guides/how-to-find-a-water-leak-in-your-house/).
3. **Get detection before demolition.** Don't let anyone start breaking concrete to "look."
4. **Ask for photos and a written report.**

Water coming through a ceiling is one of the most alarming leaks, and one of the easiest to misdiagnose. The wet spot is almost never directly under the source: water runs along joists, pipes and the back of the drywall before it finds a low point. Here's what to do right away, and how to track the water back to where it's really coming from.

## Do this first

1. **Turn off power** to the room at the breaker if water is near a light fixture, fan, smoke detector or outlet. Do it from a dry spot.
2. **Catch the water** with buckets and towels, and move furniture and electronics out of the way.
3. **Keep people and pets away from sagging drywall.** A bulging ceiling can hold a surprising amount of water and fail without warning. If it's bulging, a small hole at the lowest point, made with a screwdriver and a bucket underneath, lets the water out in a controlled way. Stand to the side.
4. **Stop the water if you know the source.** If there's a bathroom above, stop using it. If water keeps coming with nothing in use, close the main water shutoff.
5. **Take photos and video** of the ceiling, the water and any damaged belongings before you clean up, for insurance.

## Roof or plumbing? Start with the timing

| What you notice | Likely source |
|---|---|
| Appears during or after rain, or when snow melts | Roof, flashing, a skylight, or an ice dam |
| Drips only when a shower, tub, toilet or sink above is used | A **drain**, shower pan, tub overflow, wax ring or failed caulk and grout |
| Drips all the time, rain or not, fixtures off | A **pressurized supply line** in the ceiling or floor above |
| Drips in hot, humid weather when the AC runs | **Condensation** from an attic air handler, a clogged condensate line or an uninsulated duct |
| Appears after the washing machine runs | Washer supply hoses, drain hose or the washer box in the wall |

## Step 1: Do a meter test

A supply line is always under pressure, so if it's leaking, your water meter will show it.

1. Turn off every faucet, the washer, dishwasher and ice maker.
2. Note the meter reading, or watch the small leak indicator dial.
3. Wait 30 minutes to two hours without using water.
4. If the reading changed, water is escaping from a pressurized line somewhere in the house.

If the meter doesn't move, the leak is probably from a drain, a fixture that's only wet when it's used, the roof or condensation.

## Step 2: Test the fixtures above

If there's a bathroom over the stain, test one thing at a time and give each 10–15 minutes:

- **Sink:** run water into the basin, then let it drain. Check the trap and drain connections in the vanity.
- **Toilet:** flush it several times. A leak around the base after flushing points to the wax ring.
- **Tub:** fill it partway, then drain it. Leaks when draining point to the drain or overflow.
- **Shower:** spray only the walls, not the floor, to test grout and caulk. Then run water only onto the floor to test the pan and drain.

Watch the ceiling below after each test. The one that makes the stain grow or drip is your culprit.

## Step 3: Look above and around

- **Attic:** check for wet insulation, stains on the roof deck, and the condensate pan and drain line under an attic furnace or air handler.
- **Around the stain:** water can travel several feet along a joist. Look for the edge of the stain that's darkest and wettest; that's usually closest to where the water is entering.
- **Exterior:** in a two-story home, check the siding, windows and any deck or balcony above the leak.

## Step 4: Pinpoint it before opening the ceiling

Cutting drywall in the wrong spot just adds a bigger patch to the repair. Professional [hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) narrows it down first:

- **Thermal imaging** shows the cool pattern of wet drywall, or the warm trail of a hot water line, across the ceiling surface.
- **Moisture mapping** confirms how far the water has spread and where it's wettest.
- **Acoustic listening** picks up a pressurized supply line leak inside the ceiling or floor.
- **Flood testing** checks whether a shower pan holds water.

With the source marked, the plumber opens one small section. A [thermal imaging inspection](/services/thermal-imaging-inspection/) with a photo report also documents the damage for your insurer. ## Drying out and mold

The U.S. EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within **24–48 hours** to prevent mold growth ([EPA mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold)). Insulation above a wet ceiling holds water for a long time, so once the leak is fixed, wet insulation and badly soaked drywall usually need to come out rather than dry in place. Fans and a dehumidifier help the rest.

Most homeowners policies treat sudden, accidental water damage differently from long-term seepage, so report it quickly and keep your photos and any leak detection report. See [does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks](/guides/does-insurance-cover-slab-leaks/) for how coverage usually works.

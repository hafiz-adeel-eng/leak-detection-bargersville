A slab leak is a broken water line under your home's concrete foundation, and the repair bill depends less on the leak itself than on **getting to it**. Here's what slab leak repair typically costs, what pushes the price up or down, and how to keep it as low as possible.

## Typical costs

| Repair | Typical range (national) |
|---|---|
| **Spot repair** through the slab (one leak) | about $1,500–$3,500 |
| **Average single slab leak repair** | about $2,000–$4,000 |
| **Reroute** the line through walls or attic | about $600–$7,500, depending on length and route |
| **Whole-house repipe** | often $4,500–$15,000; see [repipe cost](/guides/whole-house-repipe-cost/) |

*Ranges are from national cost guides such as [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-foundation-slab-leak-repair-cost.htm) and [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/slab-leak-repair-cost). Prices in Johnson County vary; get written quotes.*

## What drives the price

- **Access:** cutting and restoring concrete, and especially **tile or hardwood** on top, is often the biggest cost.
- **Where the leak is:** under a kitchen island or cabinets costs more than in a closet or garage.
- **Hot or cold side:** hot water leaks often come with more flooring damage.
- **How many leaks:** multiple leaks push toward rerouting or repiping.
- **Pipe material and age:** old or thin copper that's pitted may not be worth patching.
- **Detection accuracy:** one small opening in the right spot versus several exploratory holes.
- **Water damage:** drying, mold prevention and flooring replacement are separate costs.

## Spot repair vs reroute vs repipe

| Option | Best when | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot repair** | One leak, line in good shape, easy flooring to restore | Least expensive; leaves the rest of the old line in place |
| **Reroute** | Older line, leak under hard-to-restore flooring, or a second leak | No floor opening; new pipe replaces the weak section |
| **Repipe** | Several leaks, thin or pitted pipe throughout | Highest cost; solves the whole system |

More detail on each option is in [slab leak repair options](/guides/slab-leak-repair-options/).

## How accurate detection saves money

The expensive version of a slab leak repair looks like this: the plumber opens the floor where the water shows up, doesn't find the leak, and opens it again somewhere else. Water travels under a slab, so the wet spot is often not above the leak.

[Slab leak detection](/services/slab-leak-detection/) first confirms which line is leaking, traces its route under the floor, and pinpoints the leak with acoustic listening and thermal imaging. The plumber then opens **one small section**, or decides with confidence that rerouting makes more sense. Detection is usually priced separately; see [what affects leak detection cost](/leak-detection-cost/).

## What insurance usually pays

Many homeowners policies pay for:

- **Water damage** to flooring, walls and belongings
- **Access**: breaking and restoring the concrete and flooring to reach the leak

Many don't pay to **fix the pipe itself**, and they may exclude long-term seepage. A detection report with the location, cause and date helps. See [does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks](/guides/does-insurance-cover-slab-leaks/).

## Keep the cost down

1. **Act fast.** A running slab leak adds water, energy and damage costs every day.
2. **For a hot-side leak,** close the cold inlet on the water heater and turn it off until the repair.
3. **Get the leak pinpointed** before any concrete is cut.
4. **Get two or three written quotes** that list access, repair, restoration and warranty.
5. **Ask about rerouting** if the line is old, the flooring is expensive, or it's not the first leak.

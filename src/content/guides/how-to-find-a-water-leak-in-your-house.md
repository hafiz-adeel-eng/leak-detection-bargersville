A hidden water leak rarely announces itself. It shows up as a water bill that creeps up, a floor that feels warm, or a faint hiss at night. The good news: you can confirm a leak and narrow down where it is in under an hour, with no tools beyond a flashlight and some food coloring.

The U.S. EPA estimates that household leaks waste nearly **10,000 gallons of water a year** in the average home, and that about **1 in 10 homes has leaks wasting 90 gallons or more per day** ([EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week)). Most of those leaks are fixable once you know where they are.

## Step 1: Do the water meter test

The meter test is the single most reliable way to confirm a leak.

1. **Turn off all water use.** Faucets, showers, the dishwasher, the washing machine, the ice maker and the irrigation system.
2. **Find your water meter.** In Bargersville and most of Johnson County, it's usually in a pit near the street or sidewalk, with a lid you can lift. Some homes have it in the basement or utility room.
3. **Write down the reading**, or take a photo. Many meters also have a small triangle or dial called a *leak indicator* that spins with even a tiny flow.
4. **Wait one to two hours** without using any water. The EPA recommends a two-hour test.
5. **Check again.** If the reading changed or the leak indicator moved, water is going somewhere.

> **Tip:** Run the test overnight or while you're at work for an even clearer result.

## Step 2: Check the toilets first

Toilets are the most common source of silent leaks. A worn flapper lets water trickle from the tank into the bowl all day.

- Put a few drops of food coloring in the **tank** (not the bowl).
- Wait 10 minutes without flushing.
- If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking.

Also listen for the tank refilling on its own when nobody has flushed. That "phantom flush" is another giveaway.

## Step 3: Look under sinks and at every fixture

Work through the house room by room with a flashlight:

| Where to look | What to check |
|---|---|
| Kitchen and bathroom sinks | Supply lines, shutoff valves and the drain trap for drips or water stains in the cabinet |
| Faucets and showerheads | Drips after they're shut off |
| Water heater | Puddles at the base, a dripping relief valve discharge pipe, damp fittings at the top |
| Washing machine | Rubber supply hoses bulging, cracked or wet at the connections |
| Refrigerator | The ice maker line behind the fridge |
| Water softener | Water running to the drain when it isn't regenerating |
| Outdoor spigots | Drips, and water pooling near the foundation |

If you find a drip, fix it and repeat the meter test. If the meter still moves, keep going.

## Step 4: Split the house in half with the shutoff valves

This step tells you whether the leak is **inside** or **outside** the house.

1. Close the **main shutoff valve** where the water line enters the house.
2. Watch the meter again.
3. **Meter still moving?** The leak is in the buried service line between the meter and your house. See [how to find an underground water leak](/guides/how-to-find-an-underground-water-leak/).
4. **Meter stops?** The leak is inside the house: in the plumbing, a fixture, or under the slab.

You can narrow it further with the **cold water inlet valve on the water heater**. Close it and retest. If the meter stops, the leak is likely on the **hot water side**, which often points to a slab leak or a line inside a wall.

## Step 5: Look for signs of a hidden leak

If every fixture checks out but the meter still moves, the leak is hidden. Walk the house and look for:

- **Warm spots on the floor**, especially on tile or hardwood. This is a classic sign of a [hot water slab leak](/guides/how-to-find-a-slab-leak/).
- **Stains, bubbling paint or soft drywall**, often below a bathroom or beside a shower. See [how to find a water leak inside a wall](/guides/how-to-find-a-leak-in-a-wall/).
- **Musty smells** in one room or closet.
- **The sound of running water** when everything is off. It's easiest to hear at night.
- **Cracks in flooring or baseboards that swell.**

## When to call a professional

Call for professional leak detection when:

- the meter test confirms a leak but you can't see it,
- you suspect a leak under the concrete slab or inside a wall,
- the leak is on the hot water side, or
- water is reaching flooring, cabinets or the foundation.

A leak detection specialist uses pressure testing, [acoustic listening and thermal imaging](/guides/leak-detection-methods/) to mark the leak's location, so your plumber opens one small area instead of guessing.

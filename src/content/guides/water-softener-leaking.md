Water softeners are everywhere in central Indiana. According to the U.S. Geological Survey, groundwater in Indiana is usually **very hard**, above **180 mg/L** as calcium carbonate ([USGS on water hardness](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water)), and softeners protect water heaters, fixtures and pipes from scale. But they're also a plumbing appliance full of fittings, O-rings and a drain line, and when one leaks, it's usually in a basement, garage or utility room where nobody looks. Here's how to find the leak and fix it.

## First: put it in bypass

The **bypass valve** sends water around the softener so the house still has water.

1. **Engage the bypass**: push or pull the knob, or turn the lever, on the softener head.
2. **Unplug the softener** if it's in the middle of a regeneration cycle.
3. **Dry everything**: the softener head, the tanks, the fittings and the floor.
4. Take it back out of bypass and **watch where water appears**.

## Where softeners leak

| Where it's wet | Likely cause | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Bypass valve** on the head | Worn or dry O-rings | Replace and lubricate O-rings with silicone grease |
| **Inlet or outlet connections** | Loose or corroded fittings, flexible connectors | Tighten or replace the fittings |
| **Top of the resin tank** | Cracked head, loose head-to-tank seal | Reseat or replace the valve head seal |
| **Bottom of the resin tank** | Crack in the tank | Replace the resin tank |
| **Drain line** | Loose clamp, line out of the drain, clogged drain | Secure and clear the line |
| **Brine tank overflow** | Softener not drawing brine; stuck float | Clean the injector; check the float and brine line |
| **Only during regeneration** | Drain line or valve problem during the cycle | Service the control valve |

Water softener leaks around the **top** usually come from the head, bypass valve or fittings. Leaks at the **bottom** are more often the tank itself or water running down from above, so check above first.

## When it's not the softener

Softeners usually sit next to the **water heater**, the **main shutoff** and the **water line entering the house**. If the softener stays dry but the floor doesn't:

- Check the **water heater** and its relief valve. See [water heater leaking](/guides/water-heater-leaking/).
- Check the **main line** and shutoff where they come through the wall or floor.
- Check any **washer** or **laundry sink** nearby.
- Do a **meter test** with everything off and the softener in bypass. If the meter still moves, a pressurized pipe is leaking somewhere, possibly under the slab or in a wall.

A softener regenerating at night makes a normal running-water sound. If you hear running water long after the cycle, see [hear water running when nothing is on](/guides/water-running-sound-in-pipes/).

## Protect the space

- Make sure the **drain line** is secured with an **air gap** above the drain so it can't slip out or siphon back.
- Put a **leak sensor** on the floor next to the softener and water heater.
- If the softener is in a basement, check that the **floor drain** works.

## Check for hidden damage

A softener in a finished basement, a closet or a slab-on-grade utility room can leak into walls and flooring you can't see.

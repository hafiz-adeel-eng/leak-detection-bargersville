Many older homes in central Indiana, including historic houses around downtown Franklin and older neighborhoods in Greenwood, were plumbed with **galvanized steel** pipe. It was the standard for decades. The problem is that it corrodes from the inside, and most of it is now well past its expected life. Here's how to identify it, what failing galvanized looks like, and what replacing it involves.

## How to identify galvanized pipe

- **Color:** dull silver-gray; may look darker or rusty with age
- **Joints:** **threaded** fittings, not soldered or crimped
- **Magnet test:** a magnet **sticks** to galvanized steel; it doesn't stick to copper, PEX or other plastic
- **Scratch test:** scratching with a key shows silvery metal, not copper color

Check where the main line enters the house, the pipes near the water heater, and any exposed lines in the basement or crawl space. Some homes have a mix: galvanized in older sections and copper or PEX where repairs were made.

## Signs galvanized pipe is failing

- **Low water pressure** that has gotten worse over the years, especially upstairs. Rust and scale narrow the inside of the pipe. See [low water pressure](/guides/low-water-pressure-leak/).
- **Rusty or brown water**, especially after the water has sat
- **Leaks at threaded joints**, where the pipe is thinnest
- **Uneven pressure** between fixtures
- **Repeated repairs** in different places

## The lead concern

Galvanized pipe itself was zinc-coated, and older coatings could contain small amounts of lead. The bigger concern is **galvanized pipe downstream of a lead service line**: it can collect lead in its corrosion scale and release it into tap water for years, even after the lead line is replaced. Federal lead and copper rules now treat galvanized lines that were downstream of lead as needing replacement.

If you have galvanized pipe in an older home:

- **Ask your water utility** what your service line is made of. Indiana American Water has a program to replace customer-owned lead service lines it finds during its lead line replacement work ([Indiana American Water lead service FAQs](https://amwater.com/inaw/water-quality/lead-and-drinking-water/lead-service-faqs)).
- **Consider testing your water** for lead with a certified lab.

## Replacement options

| Scope | When |
|---|---|
| **Replace the service line** (meter to house) | Galvanized or lead line from the street. See [water line replacement cost](/guides/water-line-replacement-cost-indiana/). |
| **Replace exposed runs** in basement or crawl space | Short-term improvement; walls still have old pipe |
| **Whole-house repipe** | The usual long-term fix. See [whole house repipe cost](/guides/whole-house-repipe-cost/). |

Most galvanized replacements use **PEX** because it's less expensive and needs fewer wall openings. Copper is also an option.

## Costs

Galvanized replacement is usually quoted as a repipe. National cost guides put a whole-house repipe at about **$4,500 to $15,000**, with PEX at the lower end and copper at the higher end, plus drywall repair. Older homes with plaster walls can cost more to access and patch.

## Leaks in older homes

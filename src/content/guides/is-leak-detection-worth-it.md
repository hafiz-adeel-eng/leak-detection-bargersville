"Do I really need to pay someone just to *find* a leak?" It's a fair question. The answer depends on one thing: whether you can see the leak. If you can, you probably don't. If you can't, detection is usually what keeps the whole job small.

## What you're really comparing

Without detection, a hidden leak is found by opening things up until the water turns up. With detection, it's found by testing and listening first.

| | Searching by opening things up | Non-invasive leak detection |
|---|---|---|
| How the leak is found | Break tile, cut drywall, dig trenches | Meter test, pressure test, acoustic, thermal, tracer gas |
| Openings | Often several, some in the wrong place | One small opening at the marked spot |
| Collateral repairs | More flooring, concrete, drywall and landscaping | Minimal |
| Time without a kitchen, bathroom or yard | Longer | Shorter |
| Documentation for insurance | Whatever photos you take | Photos, readings and a marked location |

## Where detection pays off most

- **Slab leaks.** Concrete and tile are expensive to break and replace. One accurate opening instead of several makes the biggest difference here. See [slab leak detection](/services/slab-leak-detection/).
- **Leaks inside walls and showers.** Tile walls and finished drywall are costly to open blind. See [hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/).
- **Buried water lines.** Trenching a whole line through a lawn, driveway or landscaping is a big job. See [underground leak detection](/services/underground-water-leak-detection/).
- **Pools.** Leaks in buried pool plumbing are almost impossible to find without testing each line. See [pool & spa leak detection](/services/pool-spa-leak-detection/).

## When you don't need it

Save your money when:

- the leak is **visible** — a drip, a puddle under a sink, a running toilet,
- the source is **obvious** and easy to reach, or
- a simple [meter test and fixture check](/guides/how-to-find-a-water-leak-in-your-house/) finds it.

## The hidden cost of waiting

A slow leak rarely stays small. Water soaks subfloors, drywall and insulation, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within 24–48 hours to help prevent mold. The longer a hidden leak runs, the bigger the repair and the more water is wasted. The EPA estimates household leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons a year in the average home ([EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week)).

If you're adding water every few days, the first question is simple: is the pool **leaking**, or is it just **evaporating**? Here's how to answer that, then narrow down where the leak is.

## Signs your pool is leaking

- You top up the water **more than once or twice a week**
- The water level **stops at the same point** every time, often a fitting, light or the skimmer
- **Wet or soft spots** in the yard near the pool or equipment pad
- **Cracks** in the shell, tile line or deck
- **Loose tiles**
- **Algae coming back** soon after treatment, as fresh water dilutes the chemicals
- **Air in the system** or the pump losing prime

## Step 1: The bucket test

1. Fill a bucket with pool water to about 1 inch from the top.
2. Set it on the pool's first or second step so it sits in the pool water and matches its temperature.
3. Turn off any automatic refill.
4. Mark the water level **inside the bucket** and the pool level on the **outside** of the bucket.
5. Run the pump on its normal schedule and wait **24 hours**.
6. Compare the two drops.

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| Pool drops about the same as the bucket | Evaporation. No leak. |
| Pool drops **more** than the bucket | The pool is leaking. |

## Step 2: Pump on vs. pump off

Repeat the bucket test twice: once with the pump **off** for 24 hours, and once with it **running** for 24 hours.

| Loss is greater… | Likely location |
|---|---|
| With the pump **on** | Pressure side: return lines or equipment after the pump |
| With the pump **off** | Suction side: skimmer or main drain lines, or the shell |
| The same either way | Most likely the shell, a light niche or a fitting |

Also watch where the level **stops dropping**. If it stops just below the skimmer, the skimmer is a prime suspect. If it stops at the light, check the light niche.

## Step 3: The dye test

With the pump **off** and the water still:

1. Put on goggles and get in the pool, or work from the deck for shallow spots.
2. Squeeze a small amount of leak-detection dye, or food coloring, near a suspected spot: a crack, a fitting, the skimmer throat, a return, a light niche.
3. Watch the dye. If it's **pulled into** the spot, you've found a leak.

Test every fitting, not just the first one that looks suspicious. Pools often have more than one leak.

## Step 4: Check the equipment pad

Look for drips or wet areas at the pump, filter, heater, valves and unions. A leak at the pad is the easiest kind to fix.

## When you need professional pool leak detection

The bucket and dye tests find many shell and fitting leaks, but not leaks in **buried plumbing**. If the pump test points to the lines, a professional will:

- **pressure-test each line** (returns, skimmers, main drain) one at a time,
- **listen underwater** with a hydrophone for the sound of water being pulled through a crack, and
- **listen along buried lines** with acoustic equipment to pinpoint an underground break.

Indiana's freeze–thaw winters are hard on pools. Lines, skimmer throats and fittings that weren't fully winterized can crack, and the leak only shows after opening in spring.

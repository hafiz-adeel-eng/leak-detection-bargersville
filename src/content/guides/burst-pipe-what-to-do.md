A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons of water an hour. The damage depends almost entirely on how fast the water is shut off and how fast the break is found. Here's what to do in the first minutes, what usually causes a pipe to burst, and how it gets fixed.

## Do this right now

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** See [how to shut off the water to your house](/guides/how-to-shut-off-water-to-house/).
2. **Turn off electricity** to wet areas at the breaker, only from a dry spot.
3. **Open faucets**, starting at the lowest level, to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
4. **Turn off the water heater** if the water will be off for a while.
5. **Move belongings** and start removing water.
6. **Take photos and video** before cleanup, for insurance.
7. **Call a plumber** for the repair.

## What causes pipes to burst

| Cause | Where it happens |
|---|---|
| **Freezing** | Exterior walls, crawl spaces, garages, attics, outdoor spigots |
| **High water pressure** | Anywhere; worse above 80 psi or without a working pressure-reducing valve |
| **Corrosion** | Older copper (pinholes), galvanized steel, failing polybutylene |
| **Failed fittings** | Push-fit and compression fittings, washer and fridge connectors |
| **Physical damage** | Nails or screws through a pipe, digging, settling |
| **Water hammer** | Pipes that bang when valves close quickly |

In central Indiana, **freezing** is the leading cause in winter. A frozen pipe often doesn't leak until it thaws. See [frozen pipe leaks](/guides/frozen-pipe-leaks/).

## Signs of a hidden burst pipe

Not every burst is obvious. If a pipe breaks inside a wall, ceiling, crawl space or under a slab:

- **Rushing or hissing water** sound with everything off
- A **water meter spinning** fast
- **Sudden low pressure** at every fixture
- **Water coming from under baseboards**, through a ceiling or light fixture
- **Wet drywall, bubbling paint** or stains that spread quickly
- In a slab home, **water on the floor** with no visible source, or warm spots

## How burst pipes are repaired

| Situation | Typical repair |
|---|---|
| Short split in reachable pipe | Cut out the damaged section and splice in new pipe |
| Pinhole in copper | Repair clamp as a stopgap; cut out and replace the section |
| Burst inside a wall or ceiling | Open a small section at the break, repair, then patch |
| Under a slab | Spot repair through the floor, or reroute the line; see [slab leak repair cost](/guides/slab-leak-repair-cost/) |
| Old pipe with repeated breaks | Replace the run or [repipe the house](/guides/whole-house-repipe-cost/) |

Push-fit repair couplings are useful for a temporary fix, but have a plumber make the permanent repair.

## Finding a burst you can't see

When water is appearing but the break isn't visible, guessing means opening wall after wall. [Hidden water leak detection](/services/hidden-water-leak-detection/) narrows it down first:

- **Acoustic listening** hears the pressurized leak inside the wall or under the floor.
- **Thermal imaging** shows the wet or warm pattern the water leaves.
- **Moisture mapping** shows how far the water spread.
- **Isolation tests** show which line broke: hot, cold or the service line.

The spot is marked so only one small opening is made.

## Drying and insurance

The U.S. EPA advises drying water-damaged materials within **24–48 hours** to prevent mold ([EPA mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold)). Most homeowners policies cover **sudden** water damage from a burst pipe but not the pipe itself. See [burst pipe insurance claim in Indiana](/guides/burst-pipe-insurance-claim-indiana/) for the step-by-step claim process.

## Prevent the next one

- Disconnect hoses and protect pipes in cold spaces before winter.
- Check your water pressure; add or repair a pressure-reducing valve if it's over 80 psi.
- Replace old rubber appliance hoses with braided lines.
- Consider a leak sensor or automatic shutoff valve. See [smart water leak detectors](/guides/smart-water-leak-detectors/).

Smart leak detectors have become popular, and for good reason: an inexpensive sensor under the sink can prevent costly water damage. But homeowners often expect them to do something they can't: find a leak that's hidden. Here's what each type does, and where professional leak detection fits in.

## The three types of smart leak detection

| Type | How it works | Good at | Can't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spot sensors** | A small puck or pad sounds an alarm or sends a phone alert when water touches it | Catching drips and floods right where it sits | Detect anything that doesn't reach the sensor |
| **Flow monitors** | Read your water meter or measure flow on the main line and learn normal use | Spotting water use that never stops, which is a sign of a hidden leak | Tell you *where* the leak is |
| **Automatic shutoff valves** | A motorized valve on the main line closes when a sensor or unusual flow is detected | Stopping a burst pipe or failed hose before major damage | Locate or repair the leak |

## What smart detectors catch well

- A leaking **water heater** tank
- A burst **washing machine hose**
- A dripping **supply line or trap** under a sink
- A **refrigerator ice-maker line** leak
- **Toilet overflows**
- **Basement water** from a sump pump failure

These are sudden, visible leaks. Catching them in minutes instead of days is where sensors earn their price.

## What they can't do

- **Find a leak under the slab.** A flow monitor may flag constant flow, but it can't point to where under the concrete the pipe is leaking.
- **Find a leak inside a wall.** Unless water runs down to a sensor, it goes unnoticed.
- **Find a buried service line or irrigation leak** outside the house.
- **Tell a leak from normal use,** without a learning period, and slow drips can be too small to trigger an alert.

> **In short:** a smart detector tells you **that** you have a leak. Professional leak detection tells you **where** it is.

## Where to place spot sensors

- Under the kitchen and bathroom sinks
- Behind each toilet
- Next to the water heater
- Beside or under the washing machine
- Under or behind the refrigerator
- Near the sump pump and in the lowest spot of the basement
- Near the water softener or whole-house filter

## Your detector alerted you. Now what?

1. **Stop the water** if you see active leaking. Close the fixture's shutoff valve or the main shutoff.
2. **Find the obvious source:** sink, toilet, appliance or water heater.
3. **If nothing is visible**, do a meter test to confirm the leak. See [how to find a water leak in your house](/guides/how-to-find-a-water-leak-in-your-house/).
4. **If the meter keeps moving** with everything off and you can't see a leak, it's hidden: in a wall, under the slab or in the service line.

## Are smart leak detectors worth it?

For most homes, yes. Spot sensors near water heaters, washers and sinks are inexpensive protection. A flow monitor adds a useful early warning for hidden leaks. Just don't mistake an alert for a diagnosis: the sensor starts the search, it doesn't finish it.

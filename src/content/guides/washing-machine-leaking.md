Washing machines move a lot of water through a few rubber hoses and a drain, often in a closet or upstairs laundry room where a leak can do real damage. Research by the **Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety** found washing machine failures are among the top sources of home water damage claims, and that **supply hoses** were involved in more than half of them. Here's how to find where your washer is leaking and fix it.

## Find the source

Dry the floor, then run a short cycle and watch.

| When it leaks | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| **Any time**, even when off | **Supply hose** or **valve** behind the washer |
| **While filling** | Supply hose connection or the fill valve inside the washer |
| **While draining** | **Drain hose**, **standpipe** overflowing, or a clogged drain |
| **During the spin or wash** | **Drain pump**, internal hoses, or the **tub seal** |
| **From the front** (front loader) | **Door gasket** torn or dirty |
| **Suds on the floor** | Too much or the wrong detergent |

## Supply hoses: check these first

- Look for **bulges, cracks** or wetness at the hose ends.
- Check the **washers** inside the hose couplings.
- **Replace rubber hoses every five years**, even if they look fine. The IBHS found failure rates rise sharply after five years, and the average failed hose was nearly nine years old.
- Upgrade to **braided stainless steel** hoses.
- Leave a few inches of clearance so the washer doesn't crush the hoses against the wall.
- Consider turning the valves **off** when you're away for more than a day.

## Drain problems

- The **drain hose** should be secured in the standpipe, not just hooked over the edge.
- An **overflowing standpipe** usually means a partial clog in the drain line.
- Water backing up in a **laundry sink** during draining also points to a drain clog.

## Internal leaks

If the water comes from under the machine during the wash or spin, the cause is usually inside: the **drain pump**, an internal hose, or the **tub seal**. That's an appliance repair.

## When the washer isn't the problem

If the washer stays dry but the floor doesn't:

- Check the **washer box** in the wall, where the valves and drain connect. A leak inside the wall can run down to the floor.
- Check the **water heater**, **softener** or **utility sink** nearby. See [water heater leaking](/guides/water-heater-leaking/) and [water softener leaking](/guides/water-softener-leaking/).
- Do a **meter test** with everything off. If the meter moves, a pressurized pipe is leaking.

A leak inside the laundry wall is a [hidden leak](/guides/how-to-find-a-leak-in-a-wall/). Thermal imaging and moisture mapping find it without opening the whole wall.

## Check for hidden damage

A washer leak can spread under vinyl, laminate or tile and into the subfloor. In a **second-floor laundry**, it often shows up first as a stain on the ceiling below. See [water leaking from the ceiling](/guides/water-leaking-from-ceiling/).

Signs of hidden damage:

- **Soft or spongy floor** in front of the washer
- **Lifting or discolored** flooring
- **Swollen baseboards**
- A **musty smell**

## Prevent the next leak

- Replace hoses every **five years** with braided lines.
- Put a **leak sensor** on the floor beside the washer. See [smart water leak detectors](/guides/smart-water-leak-detectors/).
- Use a **washer pan** with a drain in upstairs laundries.
- Know where the washer's **shutoff valves** are.
